About Me

Competitive programmer turning into a Machine Learning engineer

I am Omar Ehab Elgedawy, a Computer Science student majoring in Artificial Intelligence at the Arab Academy for Science, Technology and Maritime Transport (AAST) — Alamein Campus, where I study on a full scholarship. With a deep passion for algorithms, data structures, and problem-solving, I have dedicated years to mastering competitive programming and software engineering.

As a Codeforces Expert ranked in the top 5% globally, I have solved over 2,500 problems across various online judges. My competitive journey includes qualifying for the Arab and African Collegiate Programming Contest (ACPC) 5 times, achieving 6th place in 2024, earning 2 Bronze Medals at the Egyptian Olympiad in Informatics (EOI), and becoming National Champion of the International Informatics Olympiad in Teams (IIOT) 2022. Most recently, my team won 1st place in Egypt at RoboCup @Home 2026, qualifying internationally — where I led the computer vision and tracking system using YOLO and ROS.

I’m now channeling my strong algorithmic foundation into Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and Data Science. I’m fascinated by how data can reveal hidden patterns and how neural networks can learn to see, understand, and predict. From building exploratory data analysis pipelines to studying the math behind gradient descent and backpropagation, I’m on a mission to bridge competitive programming thinking with real-world AI applications.

I also contribute directly to frontier AI development as an AI Trainer & Data Annotator — performing RLHF-style preference ranking, authoring complex bilingual (Arabic/English) prompts, and debugging AI-generated code to fine-tune model behavior. Beyond that, I build production software — from high-performance C++ computer vision engines to full-stack web applications with Django and PostgreSQL — and empower the next generation of programmers through mentorship and community leadership.

Digital Clock with AM/PM Indicator

May 2025

A hardware-level digital clock project combining simulation and physical prototyping. The design implements a 12-hour clock with AM/PM indicator using D flip-flops and a 555 timer, first simulated in Proteus for verification and then prototyped on a physical breadboard. The hardware implementation uses 7490 decade counters for time division, 7447 BCD-to-seven-segment decoders for display output, and custom reset detection logic at the 12-hour boundary with AM/PM toggling for full 24-hour cycle completion. This project bridges digital logic theory with hands-on hardware engineering.
